Designing Justice + Designing Spaces

For a decade, Designing Justice + Designing Spaces (DJDS) has partnered with communities most harmed by incarceration and disinvestment to imagine, design, build, and own spaces rooted in healing instead of punishment. This is the story of what we've built together — and where we're headed next.

2016 — 2026

A decade in motion

Scroll horizontally to follow the thread

DJDS emerged organically from years of hard work and practical expertise to meet an urgent need. Our founder, Deanna Van Buren, saw this firsthand through her work with the Syracuse Peacemaking Center — a project that nearly stalled due to evident disconnect between financing, funding, and real estate. She understood that bridging this gap was critical to turning new ideas into built projects. Around the same time, thanks to funding from the Fetzer Institute for a restorative justice toolkit, the name and logo that would become the foundation for the new company were born.

These pieces fell into place in 2016 with the launch of Designing Justice + Designing Spaces. Evolving an existing body of work into a practice that brought design, development, and restorative justice together, DJDS began working inside Bay Area prisons and alongside community organizers to ensure the communities most impacted by incarceration and disinvestment had both access to and a voice in creating the healing-centered spaces they deserve.

2016
Founding

DJDS Founded in Oakland

Evolving an existing body of work into a practice that brought design, development, and restorative justice together, DJDS began working inside Bay Area prisons and alongside community organizers to ensure the communities most impacted by incarceration and disinvestment had both access to and a voice in creating the healing-centered spaces they deserve. DJDS began work with Restore Oakland LLC and the Ella Baker Center to support their initiatives to end mass incarceration — a project that would become Restore Oakland, completed in 2019.

2017
Restorative Reinvestment

Hundreds of Residents Reached

Through the Pop-Up Village, DJDS and community partners serve hundreds of residents. DJDS launches what will become a flagship mobile project, using mobile architecture to turn blighted and/or vacant urban outdoor spaces into beautiful, dynamic, and regenerative community hubs that help under-resourced communities of color get access to the resources they need to thrive. DJDS received a Robert Wood Johnson Foundation grant to support the development of a pop-up resource village to uplift community culture keepers and healers, alongside an evaluation of the initiative.

2018
Re-Entry Spaces

Five Keys Mobile Classroom & Women's Mobile Refuge Trailer

Construction is completed on the Five Keys Mobile Classroom — a refurbished MUNI bus transformed into a mobile schoolhouse, complete with classroom space, a library, and computerized learning via a mobile hotspot.

2018
Leadership and Awareness

A TED Talk Propels DJDS to an International Stage

DJDS Founder Deanna Van Buren is invited to present a TED talk on what a world without prisons could look like, introducing DJDS's work to a wider audience. “Imagine a world without prisons,” Van Buren says. “And join me in creating all the things that we could build instead.”

2019
Prison Repurposing

Atlanta Center for Equity

DJDS was called to Atlanta to support Women on the Rise and the Racial Justice Action Center, along with Mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms, to lead the city's largest engagement effort to date for a capital project — the repurposing of the Atlanta City Detention Center into a Center for Equity. The process would go on to influence jail closure campaigns around the country.

2019
Expansion

New Projects and New Places

After increasing national recognition, DJDS's work shifted to reentry housing, reentry campus concepts, and partnerships with Black churches, and created the first Mobile Refuge Rooms; new projects expanded to Atlanta and Detroit. Funding from the Kendeda Fund allowed the purchase of land in Detroit next to the LOVE Building, beginning to build out the DJDS ecosystem of care.

2019
Emergent Models

DJDS Tests the Waters with Product Design

Arising from a partnership with a Black church in East Oakland and a reentry housing operator, the Mobile Refuge Room was born to cut costs while providing dignified reentry housing. After a full-scale mock-up engagement and workforce development prototyping phase, DJDS received a patent for the modular room design.

2019
Restorative Reinvestment

Restore Oakland

The Restore Oakland building opens as a permanent home to Bay Area nonprofits that cultivate restorative economics and healing justice. The community hub consists of office spaces, shared amenities and the Peace Room on the top floor, a commercial kitchen and restaurant on the ground floor, and community meeting rooms in the basement.

2019
Restorative Reinvestment

DJDS Purchases Land in Detroit

In order to meet the unmet demand for space within the LOVE Building, DJDS purchases land directly adjacent to the building, with visions of developing a restorative justice campus at Grand & 14th.

2020
Leadership and Awareness

The Spotlight is on Designing Justice + Designing Spaces

Before the global COVID-19 pandemic takes hold, DJDS is featured in The New York Times. With a vision rooted in personal experience and a passion for social change, Deanna Van Buren shares the history of innovative projects such as the Syracuse Peacemaking Project and Restore Oakland, which created restorative, healing-centered spaces as alternatives to traditional punitive environments.

2020
Leadership and Awareness

Advocating for Change in a Whole New World

DJDS's work begins going upstream, advocating and advising on policy — including meeting with the LA County Board of Supervisors to speak about the Alternatives to Incarceration report. As the COVID-19 pandemic hits, DJDS grapples with its own restorative practice and what communities really need: programs expand into new areas and flip to online community engagement.

2020
Behavioural Health

For the Love of Well-Being

DJDS expands into behavioural health with the FLOW (For The Love of Well-being) Youth Center concept paper.

2020
Youth Justice

LA County — Youth Justice Reimagined Working Group

DJDS goes upstream with the Burns Institute as a subject matter expert alongside policy experts, advocates, and service providers in the Youth Justice Working Group — LA County's effort to reimagine a caring and supportive system for youth caught up in the justice system.

2021
Expansion

Expansion into New Regions and Project Types

DJDS expands into LA and deepens its expertise in reentry housing: the Barrios Unidos headquarters for workforce development, community spaces, affordable multifamily housing and integrated service spaces; and the Robert K. Ross Center for Hope and Healing, offering affordable housing and full-scale community and health services to uplift the economic and societal well-being of formerly incarcerated, unhoused, and economically disadvantaged residents. DJDS also expands the Pop-Up Village to San Francisco in partnership with UCSF, to address infant mortality rates among Black women in the Bayview area.

2023
Specialized Housing

Crenshaw Affordable Housing

In the vibrant Leimert Park neighborhood — a historic center for Black culture and artistry in Los Angeles — DJDS supports Liberty Ecosystem, a Black-led community land trust, in developing 56 new units of affordable housing with the creation of Crenshaw Affordable Housing.

2023
Restorative Reinvestment

The LOVE Building

Modeling the restorative vision of Restore Oakland, the LOVE Building serves as a replication of a community-owned hub in Detroit. This commercial transformation establishes a long-term sanctuary for justice-oriented nonprofits, integrating professional workspaces with versatile community hubs, retail opportunities, and a dining establishment to foster local resilience.

2023
Evaluating Impact

Inaugural Post-Occupancy Evaluation

Supported by an inaugural National Endowment for the Arts grant, DJDS conducts a participatory evaluation of the Restore Oakland building — establishing our trauma-informed process for understanding the impact of the built environment on people's well-being.

2024
Community Ownership

Pop-Up Village Transitions to Community Organization

In order to fulfill the goal of establishing community ownership over the Pop-Up Village, DJDS transitions ownership of all its assets and a license for the brand to community organization Compass & Keys.

2024
Restorative Reinvestment

Safe Healing Center

In an effort to support the care-based service of helping youth heal from traumatic occurrences in LA County, the newly established Department of Youth Development partners with DJDS to launch initial demonstration projects for a healing-centered, home-like alternative placement for youth called a Safe Healing Center. The initial focus population is girls and gender-expansive youth; the first site will be operated by the women's reentry organization A New Way of Life.

2025
Leadership and Awareness

Advocating for Change at the Grasstops and Grassroots

DJDS expanded our engagement by partnering “upstream” with agencies and municipalities.

2025
Restorative Reinvestment

The Warm Landing Place

Concept design was completed for the Warm Landing Place (WLP) — a pioneering initiative led by the Justice Care & Opportunities Department under LA County's “Care First, Jails Last” vision. WLP will offer short-term transitional housing and essential voluntary services for justice-involved individuals upon release from incarceration.

2025
Restorative Reinvestment

From Harm to Health

Building on restorative justice work with a restorative justice organization in New York and Community Works in San Francisco opening in the last two years, DJDS designs a headquarters office for a restorative justice organization working with people involved in the justice system to heal from violent offenses, and a dedicated home for the Community Works network of vital services supporting people navigating court processes, incarceration, and community return — with specialized spaces for women who have experienced domestic violence and youth with incarcerated parents.

2025
Prison Redevelopment

Feasibility Study to Transform a Shuttered Prison

DJDS joins a team to conduct a comprehensive feasibility study for the redevelopment of the MCI Concord Prison in Massachusetts, providing a model process to be replicated across the country. The project would go on to win an APA planning award.

2026
Prison Redevelopment

Workshops for the Movement

Responding to a call from community organizers across the country, DJDS develops workshops for youth advocates to learn about real estate, architecture, and planning to incorporate into their own campaigns to stop new jail construction and to shut down existing facilities across the nation.

2026
Behavioral Health

Supporting a New Behavioral Health Hospital Development

In partnership with Gateways Hospital and Wakeland Development, DJDS joins a team to guide the trauma-informed design approach for a behavioral health hospital for those not able to stand trial.

What's next
“If people can't imagine something different, we'll keep building the same things. So if we want to be the architects of a different world, it starts with our imagination now. We've spent this decade proving that communities already hold the solutions—places that repair instead of punish, that care instead of surveil. We call this life-affirming design: a way of building that doesn't just avoid harm, but actively creates safety, dignity, joy, and belonging. The next fifty years will be shaped by what gets built alongside communities, not just for them. We've shown it's possible… and effective and beautiful. Now it's time to build it everywhere, and we need partners like you to help us do that.”
Deanna Van Buren Co-Founder & Executive Director, DJDS
